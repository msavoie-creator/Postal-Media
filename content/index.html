Why another one

Every other tracker picks a lane. Every other service picks for you.

There's an app for films, a different one for books, another for games, and all of them want to tell you what to try next. Content is the one shelf for all of it, and it never suggests a thing. What's on it is what you put there.

What it does that the others don't

01

All six. One shelf.

Movies, TV, books, music, podcasts and games in one library, one search, one app. Stop keeping a list for each.

02

Remember who told you.

Every item can carry the name of the person who recommended it. Filter your whole library by recommender, so when Sam asks “did you ever watch that?”, you know exactly which one they mean.

Thank them later. Or blame them.

03

To Watch → Watching → Watched.

Or To Read → Reading → Read. To Listen → Listening → Listened. To Play → Playing → Played. The verbs change with the media, and everything you're partway through gathers in one In Progress screen.

Rate anything out of five. Your stars, not the internet's.

04

Pick for me.

Can't decide? One tap and Content picks something from your own shelf, not from a catalogue of things somebody paid to put in front of you. The best recommendation engine is the one you already filled.

05

Libraries, plural. Shared, if you like.

Keep more than one: yours, the kids', the one for the cottage. And share a library with family or friends through iCloud, so everyone adds to the same shelf and nobody buys the same book twice.

06

Lists you make. Lists we made.

Your top ten. Films to show the kids. Anything you can name, ranked or not.

Or add a whole saga in one press: the Marvel films in release order or story order, every Best Picture winner, AFI's Top 100 ranked one to a hundred, Star Wars, Bond, Middle-earth, Ghibli. Numbered where it matters. Tick things off as you go.

07

Where to watch, listen, play.

Every entry links straight out to the services that have it. Decide on the sofa, tap, and you're in the right app. No searching four of them first.

08

Bring your Goodreads.

Import the CSV straight from their export. Your shelves become shelves, your ratings become ratings, your reviews become notes, and the covers are found for you.

09

Every screen you own.

iPhone, iPad, Mac and Apple TV, kept in step through your own iCloud. Add it on the train, find it on the television, where you were going to decide anyway.

10

Nothing collected. Nowhere to collect it.

No account, no login, no server of ours, no analytics. Your library lives on your device and in your own iCloud, and we can't see either. The privacy policy is short because there's nothing in it.
